Rook Dispatch · Release 4.2  — what happened and how we stop it happening again
Dispatch final report

Release 4.2 didn't push anyone out on purpose. Four responders went quiet anyway.

The 4.2 release shortened the ping wait. That turned slow answers into misses, and the routing code counted every miss as a "no". Four responders sank to the bottom of the order, and nothing in the code could bring them back. Overall acceptance recovered, so nobody looking at the dashboard saw it happen.

How it happened

Five steps from a shorter timer to four quiet phones

Misses 2% → 21.5%Slow answers ran out of time
Each miss cost −0.12The same penalty as a "no"
No way back upOnly a "yes" adds points, and low-ranked responders stop getting pings
4 responders → 0–1 pings a weekFarlight, The Undertow, Vesper, Meteor Mite
Nobody decided this4.2 actually made acceptance history count for less (weight 0.40 → 0.25). The four turned down pings at normal rates (15–29%). The shorter ping wait and the scoring rule combined in a way nobody had planned for.
The evidence

The average recovered. The four didn't.

Turned-down pings didn't rise. People weren't saying no; they weren't reaching the phone in time. The Gale and Nightwell picked up the pings the four stopped getting.

What changes in 4.3

  • A miss stops counting as a "no".Misses are recorded and scored separately from turn-downs.
  • Scores recover.Each day, a score moves about 5% of the way back toward 0.5.
  • Nobody disappears.If a responder has been available for 72 hours with no ping, they go first for the next matching callout.
  • People can see it.Meteor Mite gets a countdown on each ping, and Kip gets a rotation line on Meteor Mite's card.

Keeping it from happening again

Check the brief before it ships, and check the data after

4.2 didn't fail on code alone. Nobody had written down how routing works, so nobody could answer Marcus's question on 14 Aug ("was this meant to apply to responders who turn jobs down?"). The only number anyone watched was an average.

Before it ships

review-checklist

1
Names who owns itAfter Priya left, her name was still on every Q3 roadmap item and nobody owned the routing behaviour. The skill flags a missing owner, and an owner who has left.
2
Says how we'll know it workedIt asks for a baseline and a target. That pushes a brief toward per-responder measures, which would have shown the four, not just the 72.7% average.
3
Same scope start to finish4.2 changed two things at once: the routing weights and the ping wait. The skill names each extra change and where it first appears.
4
Problem before the fixIt makes a brief state who is affected and what goes wrong for them before proposing a fix.
Every Monday

Scheduled review

"Monday brief review" runs review-checklist on every brief that changed that week and reports back. It only reads files. Weak briefs get caught on a routine, not by luck.

1gap caught on the first run
After it ships

Weekly health check

The skill checks the writing, not the code. The health check covers the gap: it reads the pings data and goes red if any responder's pings fall below half their usual.

It's drafted and backtested. It still needs to be scheduled, and Ravi, Wen and Nadia need to agree to own their metrics.
